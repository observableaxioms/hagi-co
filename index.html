About

Nexum Labs builds alternative datasets and models for investors who need to see physical-world shifts before they show up in official statistics. We focus on where AI, energy, real estate, and natural resources collide.

I'm Victoria, an NYU Stern founder. I built Ironclad's international go-to-market motion from scratch, then spent two years in Silicon Valley researching efficient, interpretable domain-specific models for time-series and spatial data, where transformers, built around the semantic structure of language, still struggle.

Why us

The markets we cover are hard for general-purpose AI. Power demand and prices change over time and shift between regimes. Vacancies and mineral deposits depend on location, and how places relate to each other. General-purpose models, built for language, often struggle with both.

We build models designed for time and space, and we evaluate them honestly: only on data they've never seen, always against simple baselines, and with uncertainty that widens as the forecast horizon does.

Products

1. AI Compute Demand

A time-series dataset tracking how AI compute buildout translates into power demand, starting with Texas. We combine public compute and grid data with on-the-ground sources to show where demand is heading, and when.

Sources: Epoch AI, ERCOT, onsite power deployments, local developers and operators. Status: [Available / In beta]

2. U.S. Vacancy Data

A spatial dataset of commercial and residential vacancies across the United States, built to help REITs and residential investors compare markets, time acquisitions, and monitor portfolio risk at the neighborhood level.

Use cases: REIT analysis, residential investment, market screening. Status: [Available / In beta]

3. Critical Minerals Project Model

A geospatial AI model that assesses the likelihood that critical mineral projects succeed, combining geology with project and market signals to help investors decide where to commit capital.

Status: Upcoming. Join the waitlist.

How we work

Every dataset ships with documentation: what we collected, what we didn't, how often it updates, and where it shouldn't be used. Every model is tested against out-of-sample data and simple benchmarks before we make claims about it.
